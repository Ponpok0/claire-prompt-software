There's a false binary baked into that question, and it's worth pulling apart before you make any strategic shift.

First, what your data actually says versus what you're reading into it. A 2.5% interview rate from 200 applications isn't great, but it's also not unusual depending on your field and seniority level. The more interesting number is 5 interviews, zero offers. That's a conversion problem, not necessarily a volume problem. If you were getting zero interviews, the issue would be resume/targeting. But you're clearing the first filter and then failing to close. Those are two completely different problems, and "apply more" doesn't fix the second one, but neither does "only network."

Your friend's situation isn't evidence that effort doesn't work. It's evidence that referrals shorten the process, which has always been true and isn't exactly a secret. But you're taking one data point from someone else's career and using it to invalidate two months of your own strategy. That's a pretty aggressive inference from a sample size of one.

Here's where the real split is, and it's not applications versus networking. It's between two different failure modes you might be in:

**You're targeting well but interviewing poorly.** Five interviews with no offers suggests something is breaking in the conversation itself. Could be how you're framing your experience, how you're handling behavioral questions, salary expectations, energy level after two months of grinding. This is fixable, but not by changing your sourcing strategy.

**You're casting too wide and landing interviews at places that were never going to be a fit.** "Tailoring each resume" for 200 companies in two months means you're spending maybe 30 minutes per application. That's "tailored" in a superficial sense. If you're applying to anything remotely adjacent to your skills, the interviews you're getting might be low-quality matches where you were always going to lose to someone more specific.

Networking isn't the opposite of applications. It's a different entry point into the same funnel. And honestly, most people who say "I should network more" mean "I should ask people I vaguely know if they have jobs," which is the networking equivalent of mass-applying. Effective networking is slower, more specific, and harder than sending resumes, not easier.

So before you pivot strategy entirely, a couple of things worth examining. Those 5 interviews: did you get any feedback? Did they feel like genuine fits or were you stretching? And the 195 rejections: is there a pattern in which types of roles are ignoring you versus which ones responded? Because the answer to "what should I do differently" is hiding in those patterns, not in your friend's LinkedIn activity.

The "work hard and you'll be rewarded" framing is worth questioning, but not in the direction you're taking it. The issue isn't that effort is pointless. It's that effort without feedback loops is just repetition. Two hundred applications with no post-interview debrief is like practicing piano for two months without ever listening to yourself play.
