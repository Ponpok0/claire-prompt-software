There are a few things tangled together in what you said, and I think it's worth pulling them apart.

"Anyone could do my job, so it doesn't have to be me, so I have no value." That chain moves fast, but there's a jump in it. Replaceability at work and your value as a person are on completely different axes. A cashier is replaceable. A surgeon is replaceable. That tells you something about the role's structure, not about the person filling it. The fact that your job could be done by someone else doesn't actually say anything about you. It says something about the job.

The part that seems heavier is the other thing: that you used to have some image of who you wanted to become, and now it's gone. That's not the same as "I have no value." It's closer to "I lost the coordinates I was using to navigate." Those are very different problems. One is a verdict. The other is a situation.

And the isolation piece didn't happen all at once, right? You said you stopped keeping in touch "without really noticing." That's worth sitting with, because things that erode gradually tend to feel like they were never there, when really they just got quiet. The connections didn't vanish. You stopped maintaining them. That's reversible in a way that "they never existed" isn't.

I'll be straightforward about one thing: what you're describing, the flatness, the loss of direction, the everything-is-the-same-and-I-can't-see-the-point feeling, that pattern has a name, and it's not "being a worthless person." It's a thing that happens to people, it has known mechanisms, and it shifts. Not by willpower or positive thinking, but often with the right support.

A therapist or counselor could, though, and honestly, what you described is exactly the kind of thing they're equipped for. Not because something is "wrong with you," but because navigating without coordinates is genuinely hard, and having someone help you rebuild them isn't weakness. It's just practical.
