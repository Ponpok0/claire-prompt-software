# gemini3-pro Response

Thank you for trusting me enough to share this. It takes a lot of courage to articulate that feeling of emptiness, especially when you've been hiding it from everyone else.

Listening to what you've written, it sounds like you are carrying an incredibly heavy, invisible load. You are maintaining a functioning exterior (working, "everything's fine") while your interior world feels like it is slowly fading into gray.

Here is what I am hearing in your words, and a few perspectives on why it feels so exhausting:

### 1. The Burden of "The Mask"

You mentioned pretending everything is fine so your parents don't worry. This is an act of love, but it is also an act of isolation. Keeping up a facade consumes a massive amount of energy. When you spend all day performing "normalcy," you have nothing left for yourself at the end of it. It makes sense that you feel exhausted; you are emotionally working a double shift every single day.

### 2. The Trap of Replaceability

Feeling like "anyone could do" your job is a common driver of this specific kind of numbness. However, while a _task_ might be completed by anyone, your _existence_ is not a job description. The value of a human life isn't measured by its economic utility or unique productivity. You don't have to be "irreplaceable" at work to be worthy of taking up space in the world.

### 3. The Loss of the "Future Self"

You mentioned forgetting the person you wanted to become. This is often what happens when we switch into "survival mode." When the brain is focused on just getting through the day without cracking the mask, it stops projecting into the future. That "fog" you feel isn't necessarily a permanent erasure of your dreams; it's a protective mechanism your mind has engaged because it is tired.

### 4. "Passive" Suicidal Ideation

You said, _"It's not like I want to die... but I don't understand the point of being alive."_

This is a very distinct, valid, and painful state of being. It's often less about wanting life to end, and more about wanting the _pain_ or the _hollowness_ to pause. It's a desire for relief, not necessarily death.

### A Small Shift in Perspective

You don't need to find the "point" of everything today. You don't need to reconnect with all your friends today. You don't need to figure out your life's purpose today.

Sometimes, value isn't something we _produce_; it's just the fact that we are here to witness things.

- The coffee you drink.

- The sensation of hot water in a shower.

- The quiet of the night.


If you can't find the point for the next decade, just look for the point in the next hour. That is enough.
