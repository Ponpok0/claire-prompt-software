You're not being ridiculous, and you don't need to "grow up." What you're feeling is actually a sign that you're a decent person who takes loyalty seriously. That said, your wife isn't wrong either — both things can be true at the same time.

Here's what I think might help you reframe this:

Your boss did those things — protecting you during layoffs, giving you space when your dad was sick — because he's a good manager and, it sounds like, a good person. But those things were also part of a working relationship where you were presumably doing good work for him. He wasn't doing charity. He was investing in someone he valued. That's not transactional in a cold way, it's just honest. You don't owe someone your career trajectory because they were a good boss. If anything, a boss that good will probably understand.

And think about it from his side: if he got an offer tomorrow that was meaningfully better for his family and his career, would you expect him to turn it down out of loyalty to you? Of course not. You'd want him to take it. A good boss — the kind you're describing — will feel the same way about you.

As for the conversation itself, the thing that would actually feel like a betrayal is disappearing without honesty. What won't feel like a betrayal is sitting down with him, being direct, and telling him something close to what you told me: that this was a hard decision precisely because of how much his support has meant to you, that you got an opportunity you can't pass up, and that you didn't take it lightly. You don't need to over-explain or apologize. Just be genuine.

Most good bosses, when someone they respect leaves well — with honesty, gratitude, and professionalism — they don't feel betrayed. They feel proud. And the relationship doesn't have to end. Some of the best professional relationships people have are with former bosses.

The guilt you're feeling is the tax you pay for not being the kind of person who just walks away without caring. Feel it, let it inform how you handle the conversation, and then go take the job.
